## Full Summary

This evaluation re-analyzes two prior artifacts (the synthetic room-transition-vs-PreHeat thermal-simulation experiment and the real CASAS multi-room occupancy dataset) entirely read-only, computing four metric families with no new predictor training. (1) Realism/calibration: per-room occupied fraction, mean dwell time (min), and current-room-conditioned transition entropy (bits) are computed for all 3 synthetic topologies (via method.py's own generate_topology_data, imported directly with the exact recorded seeds) and all 4 real CASAS houses (Aruba/Cairo/Milan/Tulum, reconstructed from their attached room-transition tuples), then z-scored and compared via a 3x4 synthetic-to-real Euclidean distance matrix identifying the closest/farthest topology-house pairs (closest: house_5room_irregular vs tulum, z-distance ~1.8; farthest: studio_linear_regular vs tulum, z-distance ~4.1). (2) Corrected CI + power: the cross-topology bootstrap 95% CI on energy savings is recomputed at 2000 resamples (reusing method.py's own bootstrap_savings_ci) and reproduces the original n=3 CIs within Monte Carlo noise; a post-hoc power analysis (one-sample t-test MDE formula plus statsmodels TTestPower.solve_power) shows the n=3 design's minimum detectable effect is large (tens to ~156 percentage points depending on FPR) and would require tens to hundreds of independent topology conditions to reliably detect a 5-percentage-point effect at 80% power -- reframing the original 'CI includes zero' result as underpowered/inconclusive rather than a confirmed null. No companion larger-n experiment was available at eval time, logged explicitly as a fallback. (3) Graded comfort proxy: a fine-grained re-simulation (reusing method.py's init_thermal_models/thermal_step/fit_heat_rate/predictor classes verbatim, only adding bookkeeping) computes integrated temperature deficit during anticipated-but-not-yet-occupied windows and realized anticipation lead time per true occupancy onset, for both predictors across all 3 topologies x 3 matched-FPR levels, plus paired (transition-PreHeat) differences with bootstrap 95% CIs. This surfaces a mechanism invisible to the original binary MissTime metric: at every matched-FPR operating point, method.py's own threshold_at_fpr() resolves the transition predictor's threshold to 1.0, so its anticipatory-heating branch is essentially never triggered and its heating is driven almost entirely by the shared reactive-fallback rule -- a plausible root cause for the disconnect between the strong AUC advantage and the null energy-savings result, both pooled differences excluding zero in the negative direction. (4) Full AUC table: all 3-topology x 4-lookahead = 12 AUC cells recomputed from the stored ROC FPR/TPR arrays via sklearn.metrics.auc, confirming the transition predictor dominates PreHeat's ROC-AUC in 12/12 cells (mean gap +0.115, paired bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero, Wilcoxon one-sided p=2.44e-04) -- a single omnibus significance statement replacing the original prose assertion that only cited example numbers for 2 of 3 topologies. All four tables plus a narrative interpretation are written to eval_out.json (schema-validated against exp_eval_sol_out.json), with full/mini/preview variants generated.
