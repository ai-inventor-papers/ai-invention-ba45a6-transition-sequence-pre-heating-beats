## Full Summary

Full-text review of both flagged Markov-chain occupancy papers confirms neither anticipates this hypothesis: Salimi/Liu/Hammad (Building and Environment 2019) targets multi-occupant open-plan OFFICES using RTLS badge tracking, models a 5-state 'work-state' Markov chain per occupant (not a room-to-room transition predictor), and evaluates with R^2 accuracy against lighting/HVAC control (86%/68%), never PreHeat's Hamming-distance K=5 baseline or its ROC/MissTime/gas-consumption metrics. Zhang et al. (Advanced Engineering Informatics 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.aei.2025.104204) is a purely methodological continuous-time-Markov-chain paper solving a model-complexity/overfitting problem via log-likelihood on 9 months of unspecified-setting occupancy data -- it never engages PreHeat's residential comfort/energy framing either. Both gaps are real but narrow: the paper must still explicitly state that transition-sequence modeling combined with PreHeat's specific baseline and metrics, in a residential single/small-occupant setting, is what is new -- not transition modeling per se, since Markov occupancy modeling generally is a saturated technique. On the dataset side, CASAS Aruba (single resident, ~220 days, motion+door+temperature at room granularity via the 'Translate01' room-level field, freely downloadable from Zenodo, no gated request) is the strongest fit for both reproducing PreHeat's per-room baseline and fitting a room-to-room transition model with held-out days. CASAS Milan (single resident, ~72 days) is a solid secondary/held-out-model validation set. CASAS Cairo, Kyoto7, and ARAS are multi-resident with only binary/aggregate sensors and no per-person ID, so trajectories cannot be cleanly resolved -- usable only with an explicit, flagged occupant-disambiguation heuristic. Orange4Home (Inria/Orange Labs) is a genuine single-occupant, room-level (8 places, 236 sensors) apartment dataset closely matching PreHeat's own deployment style, but is access-gated (dataset obtained by emailing the author) and only ~28 days, one day short of the plan's >=30-day threshold. The classic van Kasteren dataset's original TU Delft host appears dead; only partial/unofficial CSV mirrors were found (e.g., a GitHub issue thread), so it carries real availability risk and should not be relied on without further verification. MERL's dataset is a 2007 single-sensor motion-detector/office presence corpus, not room-level residential occupancy -- confirmed unsuitable/likely-inapplicable rather than merely unfound. Overall recommendation: use CASAS Aruba as the primary driver for both the PreHeat-baseline reproduction and the transition-model experiment, with CASAS Milan as a secondary validation dataset; treat Orange4Home as a stretch goal contingent on the access-request turnaround fitting the experiment executor's time budget; and keep a PreHeat-calibrated synthetic trace generator (calibrated to PreHeat's own reported per-room occupied-fraction statistics) as an explicitly acceptable fallback if CASAS's motion-sensor-to-room aggregation proves too lossy during implementation.
